How the math works

Every number in Skuld is a simple multiplication you can check by hand. The examples below use the sample data: 50 staff, $2.5M payroll, $12M revenue, $5M COGS, an 8-hour day and 260 working days a year.

1. Downtime: the hourly rate

  • Operating hours per year = hours per day × days per year. 8 × 260 = 2,080
  • Idle payroll / hr = annual payroll ÷ operating hours × % staff affected × % productivity lost × (1 + benefits load). $2.5M ÷ 2,080 = $1,202
    Using an hourly rate instead: rate × headcount × the same percentages.
  • Lost revenue / hr = annual revenue ÷ operating hours × % revenue lost (not recovered later). Monthly revenue is × 12 first. $12M ÷ 2,080 × 50% = $2,885
  • COGS / hr = annual COGS ÷ operating hours × the same % revenue lost. Per-head COGS is × headcount first. $5M ÷ 2,080 × 50% = $1,202
    • Avoided (default): subtracted from lost revenue, giving lost gross profit. $2,885 − $1,202 = $1,683
    • Still incurred: added on top as wasted COGS.
  • Cost per hour = idle payroll + lost revenue (or gross profit) + wasted COGS. $1,202 + $1,683 = $2,885
  • Cost of the outage = cost per hour × outage hours. One "day" = one operating day (8 h by default, 24 h if you set 24 × 365). 1 day = $23,077

2. Organization profile (industry)

  • Changes only the ransom estimate and the benchmark sentence. It does not change any penalty math.
  • Ransom factor: Healthcare ×0.15 · State / local government ×2.5 · every other industry ×1.0 (no published figure for other sectors).

3. Data size helper

  • Records = GB × 1,048,576 ÷ KB per record. 1 GB ÷ 2 KB = 524,288
  • "Apply count" copies the result into the data type you pick. Skuld assumes 1 record = 1 person for every cost below.
  • There's no published benchmark for KB per record. Set it from the client's actual database.

5. HIPAA, worked through

  • Tier minimums per record: Tier 1 $145 · Tier 2 $1,461 · Tier 3 $14,602 · Tier 4 $73,011.
  • Example: 8,000 health records × $1,461 = $11.7M → hits the cap → $2,190,294. For anything bigger than a small breach, the cap decides the number.
  • Each extra provision violated raises the cap by another $2.19M.

6. Ransom, worked through

  1. Annual revenue picks the size band. $12M → "under $50M": $140K demand, $50K payment.
  2. Multiply by the industry factor. General ×1.0 → $50,000; healthcare ×0.15 → $7,500.
  3. Typing your own amount replaces the estimate. The full amount always counts toward the total; Skuld measures exposure, not odds.

7. The cross-check line (not added to the total)

  • Records × IBM's all-in cost per record: $160 customer, $168 employee, $178 IP, $154 corporate, $115 anonymized. 5,000 × $160 + 50 × $168 = $808,400
  • IBM's figure already includes lost business, which the downtime side covers. Compare it to downtime + incident together. If your total is far below it, your inputs are probably too light.

Know the gaps

  • IP, other corporate and anonymized data add $0 to the total; no per-record fee applies. For an IP-heavy client, raise Recovery or use Other fines.
  • HIPAA is worst case. It treats each record as one violation; regulators often settle lower.
  • CCPA uses the statutory minimum ($107). The range runs to $799; class-action payouts are usually lower.
  • Health records are left out of CCPA, because California exempts HIPAA-covered data.
  • Excluded entirely: overtime, SLA penalties, customer churn and reputational damage.
